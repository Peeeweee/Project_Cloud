The full cost. Not just the fee.

In one line: the price tag is not the whole cost.

STARTTitle

"Thanks, Renan. Paulo showed us how to keep the system running. Renan showed us how to protect it. My part is about money: how much the cloud really costs, and how we keep that cost under control."

NEXTCapEx vs OpEx

"There are two ways to pay for computers. CapEx means you buy everything upfront, like buying your own servers. OpEx means you pay over time, like renting. The cloud is mostly OpEx: you rent and pay as you use."

"But look at the line below: the cloud changes WHEN you pay, not automatically HOW MUCH. Renting is not always cheaper. It just moves the payment."

NEXT4 boxes: Compute · Storage · Transfer · People

"To see the real cost, we add up four things.

Compute: the machines, times how long they run.
Storage: your data, the requests, and the backups.
Transfer: moving data from one place to another.
People: the time it takes to run, secure, and fix everything."

NEXT"A cheap server can hide expensive transfer and staff time."

"So a server with a cheap price tag can still end up expensive, because of data transfer or the hours people spend taking care of it. That's why we compare the full cost, not just the fee."

If someone asks

"So is the cloud always cheaper?"

"No. For a big system that runs the same way all day, every day, owning can sometimes be cheaper. You have to compare the full cost over the same period."

"What's TCO?"

"Total cost of ownership: every cost added together, not just the price tag."

273 Next clicks · about 1 min 20 s

A lower rate. A bigger bill?

In one line: commit if you use it a lot; pay as you go if you don't.

STARTTitle

"Cloud providers give you two main ways to pay. Which one is cheaper? It depends on how much you actually use."

NEXTAlways on · 730 hours

"Our example uses ten cents per hour. A month has about 730 hours.

On-Demand means you pay only for the hours you use. If the server runs all month, that's 730 × $0.10 = $73.

Commitment means you promise to pay for every hour of the month, and in return you get 40% off. That's $43.80.

If the server runs all the time, commitment wins."

NEXTPart time · 176 hours

"But what if the server only runs part of the time, say 176 hours, like during school or office hours?

On-Demand: 176 × $0.10 = $17.60.
Commitment: still $43.80, because you promised to pay for every hour, even the hours you don't use.

Now On-Demand wins. A lower rate gave us a bigger bill."

NEXTBreak-even · 438 hours

"So where's the line? $43.80 ÷ $0.10 = 438 hours, about 60% of the month.

If you use it more than that, commit. If you use it less, just pay as you go."

If someone asks

"What's Spot?" (it was a game word)

"Spot is very cheap spare capacity, but the provider can take it back anytime. It's only good for work that can be stopped and restarted."

"Are these real prices?"

"No, they're simple example numbers to show the idea. Real prices change."

284 Next clicks · about 1 min

Meet the peak. Then let go.

In one line: grow when it's busy, shrink when it's quiet.

STARTTitle

"Elastic, one of our game words. Elastic means the system can stretch when it's busy and shrink back when it's quiet, like a rubber band."

NEXTA normal day · 2 machines

"Imagine an online shop. On a normal day, two machines are enough."

NEXTSale day · 18 more for 24 hours

"Then there's a big sale, and lots of customers come at once. We add 18 more machines, so 20 in total, but only for one day."

NEXTSale over · release the extra 18

"When the sale ends, we let go of the extra 18 and go back to two. We stop paying for them."

NEXTThe monthly bill

"Here's the difference in one month:

Elastic (grow, then shrink): $189.20.
Fixed (keep 20 machines all month just in case): $1,460.

Same peak, but almost 8 times cheaper. Scaling down is part of the saving."

If someone asks

"How did you get $189.20?"

"Two machines all month: 2 × 730 × $0.10 = $146. Eighteen extra for 24 hours: 18 × 24 × $0.10 = $43.20. Add them: $189.20."

"What's the difference between scalability and elasticity?"

"Scalability means it can grow. Elasticity means it grows and shrinks back automatically to match demand."

293 Next clicks · about 1 min

Give every resource an owner.

In one line: every cost needs a name next to it.

STARTTitle

"Now, who is responsible for the bill? In the cloud, it's easy to create things and forget them. So every resource needs an owner."

NEXTTag card · web-server-02 · shop-team · online-shop · test

"This is a tag, like a name label. This server is called web-server-02. It belongs to the shop team, it's for the online shop project, and it's only a test server.

Without tags, you just see a charge and nobody knows whose it is. A tag turns a mystery charge into a named owner."

NEXTInform → Optimize → Operate

"This way of managing cloud money is called FinOps: finance and tech teams working together. It's a loop of three steps:

Inform: see where the money goes.
Optimize: find ways to spend less without breaking anything.
Operate: actually do it, check the results, and repeat."

NEXT"A budget alert is not a brake."

"One important warning: a budget alert only tells you that you're spending too much. It does not stop the spending. It's like a fuel warning light in a car: it warns you, but it doesn't stop the car. Someone still has to act."

If someone asks

"What's a quota?" (it was a game word)

"A quota is a limit on how much of something you're allowed to use. It's different from a budget alert, which only warns you."

301 Next click · about 1 min

One system. Every layer matters.

In one line: all three parts protect the same system.

STARTTitleNEXTThe whole picture

"Let's put all three parts together in one picture.

Visitors come in through HTTPS, the safe, locked web door. They reach the application, and only the application can talk to the private database. The public can't reach it directly.

Administrators use a separate, controlled path, not the same door as visitors. That's Renan's part.

Monitoring, audit logs, and backups help us see problems and recover. That's Paulo's part.

Budgets and owners keep spending in check. That's my part.

If one layer is weak, the whole system is weak. Observe it. Protect it. Account for it."

A key leaks. What comes first?

In one line: block the leaked key first, then clean up.

STARTTitleNEXTChoices A · B · C

"Let's test you. Imagine this: someone's cloud key, which is like a password for the cloud account, gets leaked online. Suddenly there are machines we don't know about, and our bill is going up fast.

What should we do first? A, B, or C?"

Let the class answer. Click each choice to show its explanation.

  • A Increase the budget ✗
    "No. More budget doesn't stop the attacker. It just lets them spend more."
  • C Delete the repository file ✗
    "Not enough. The attacker already copied the key. Deleting the file doesn't make the key stop working."
  • B Contain the credential ✓
    "Correct! First, block the leaked key so it can't be used anymore. Then investigate what happened, remove the unknown machines, and recover."

"And notice: this one problem touches all three parts. Security, because a key leaked. Operations, because we have to investigate and recover. Economics, because the bill went up."
